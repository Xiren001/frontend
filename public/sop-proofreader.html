Role Overview

As a Proofreader at Faszik, your primary responsibility is ensuring that all product copy — ads, landing pages, and product descriptions — meets the linguistic and quality standards of your assigned language before products advance through the build pipeline.

Your reviews are a critical quality gate. A product cannot move from the Proofread phase to the Testing phase without a completed proofread sign-off.

💡
Your Scope
You only see products assigned to your language. This is by design — stay focused on your language queue and do not attempt to access other language queues.

Key Responsibilities

  • Review all copy submitted to your language's proofread queue daily
  • Check grammar, spelling, punctuation, and tone
  • Verify copy is culturally appropriate and natural-sounding for native speakers
  • Flag issues clearly with specific notes so the ads/web team can action them
  • Approve or reject each item with a clear written rationale
  • Maintain a turnaround time of 24 hours per item in queue

Daily Workflow

Morning Routine (Start of Shift)

  1. Log in to Myko Ops Hub and navigate to Proofread Queue.
  2. Check the queue depth indicator — note how many items are pending review.
  3. Sort by submission date — oldest first. Process in FIFO order unless flagged as urgent.
  4. For each item: open the copy, review against the standards in Section 4, then approve or flag.

Processing Each Item

  1. Open the product entry in the queue.
  2. Read the full copy from start to finish without editing.
  3. Re-read and note any issues (grammar, spelling, tone, cultural fit).
  4. Navigate to Copy Review to view the structured copy fields.
  5. Add line-by-line comments for each issue found — be specific (e.g., "Line 3: 'kaufen' should be 'erwerben' for formal tone").
  6. Set the status to Approved or Needs Revision.
  7. Submit the review. The build pipeline will update automatically.

4

Review Standards

Apply the following checklist to every piece of copy you review. Each item must pass before you can approve.

Grammar & Spelling

  • No spelling errors — use your language's standard dictionary
  • Correct verb conjugation and tense consistency throughout
  • Proper noun capitalization per language rules
  • Correct use of articles (where applicable to the language)
  • Punctuation follows language-specific conventions (e.g., spacing before/after punctuation in French)

Tone & Style

  • Tone matches the product type — persuasive but not pushy for ads; informative for product pages
  • Consistent formality level throughout (formal vs. informal "you" must not mix)
  • No awkward literal translations — copy should read as if written by a native speaker
  • Avoid clichés or overly generic phrases
  • Calls to action are clear, natural, and compelling

Cultural Fit

  • References, idioms, and examples are culturally relevant
  • Pricing references, units, and formats are localized (€ vs $, comma vs period for decimals)
  • No culturally insensitive language
  • Seasonal or timely references are appropriate for the target market

Technical Accuracy

  • Product names and brand names are spelled correctly and consistently
  • URLs, discount codes, or special instructions are not altered
  • Placeholder text (e.g., [PRODUCT NAME]) has been properly replaced

Language-Specific Guidelines

Beyond the universal standards above, apply these language-specific considerations:

AreaGuideline
Formal vs. InformalEstablish the correct register for each language (e.g., "Sie" vs "du" in German, "vous" vs "tu" in French) and enforce it consistently
Gender AgreementFor gendered languages, all adjectives, articles, and pronouns must agree with the noun's gender
Character LimitsSome languages expand significantly in translation — flag if copy is too long for its intended format (e.g., ad headline limits)
Local IdiomsReplace English idioms with natural-sounding local equivalents rather than direct translations
Numbers & DatesVerify date formats and number separators match local standards
Legal TermsDo not alter any legal disclaimers or guarantee language — flag for admin review if they appear incorrect

Escalation Process

Not every issue can be resolved through the standard review workflow. Use these escalation paths when needed:

When to Escalate

  • Copy contains potential legal liability (false claims, misleading guarantees)
  • Product description is factually incorrect about the product itself
  • Copy has already been revised twice and still fails quality standards
  • You are unsure whether content is culturally appropriate — when in doubt, escalate
  • Queue contains 10+ items — notify your manager to avoid pipeline blockage

Escalation Steps

  1. Add a detailed note in the Copy Review comments explaining the issue and why you are escalating.
  2. Set the item status to Escalated (if available) or Needs Revision with "ESCALATE:" prefix in comments.
  3. Notify your team lead directly (message or email) with the build ID and a brief description of the issue.
  4. Do not hold up other queue items while waiting for escalation resolution. Continue with the rest of the queue.
🚨
Never Approve Uncertain Copy
If you have any doubt about copy quality or appropriateness, do not approve it. It is better to delay a build than to approve copy that could harm brand reputation or violate regulations.
